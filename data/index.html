Battery DPP — Regulatory Overview

Key requirements under Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 for Digital Product Passports on batteries placed on the EU market.

1

What is Battery DPP?

A standardized digital record (accessed via a physical QR code) that stores data on a battery’s identity, performance, sustainability, material composition, and health status across its entire life cycle.

Article 77 & 78
2

When does it take effect?

February 18, 2027 Article 77(1)

All covered batteries placed on the EU market after this date must have an active DPP.

3

Which batteries require a DPP?

Mandatory for 3 battery categories Article 77(1):

  • LMT Batteries E-bikes, e-scooters & similar LMT vehicles — Light Means of Transport (e.g., e-bikes, e-scooters)
  • EV Batteries Electric cars & other electric vehicles — Electric Vehicles
  • Industrial Batteries Stationary storage & industrial applications (> 2 kWh) — Capacity > 2 kWh (includes stationary energy storage systems)
4

Who is impacted across the value chain?

Manufacturers
Create, register, and fund the initial DPP.
Importers & Distributors
Verify DPP compliance before placing products on the EU market.
Repurposers / Remanufacturers
Must update DPP data during second-life operations Articles 73–74.
Recyclers & Service Operators
Access battery chemistry and health data (SoH) for safe dismantling and recycling.
Customs & Market Surveillance
Validate digital compliance at EU borders.
Consumers
Gain transparency on carbon footprint and sustainability metrics.
5

How is it implemented?

Architecture: Scannable QR code linked to an open, machine-readable, interoperable EU digital registry Article 78.

Role-Based Data Access (Annex XIII)

  • Public General specs, carbon footprint, recycled content, CE certificate.
  • Restricted (Technicians / Recyclers) State of Health (SoH), electrical diagrams, dismantling manuals.
  • Authorities Compliance audit reports, Supply Chain Due Diligence details.
6

Why DPP is necessary?

  • 🌿
    Circular Economy Enables battery second-life applications and efficient critical material recovery.
  • 📉
    Decarbonization Forces transparency and reduction of manufacturing carbon footprints.
  • 🛡️
    Supply Chain Integrity Enforces ethical raw material sourcing (Due Diligence).
  • ⚖️
    Market Enforcement Provides EU Customs with tools to instantly block non-compliant imports.
7

What quantitative thresholds are tracked?

Recycled Content Minimums (by 2031)

Mandatory percentage of recovered materials in new batteries:

  • Cobalt16%
  • Lead85%
  • Lithium6%
  • Nickel6%

Recycling Efficiency Targets

Lithium-based battery recycling efficiency target of 65% by 2025, rising to 70% by 2030.

Carbon Footprint Classes

Emission ratings (A–D performance classes) determining market access.

6 Lifecycle Stages

  1. Raw Material Mining & Extraction
  2. Material Refining & Chemical Processing
  3. Cell Manufacturing & Pack Assembly
  4. Market Distribution, Sales, and Usage
  5. Second-Life, Remanufacturing, or Repurposing
  6. End-of-Life (EoL), Collection, and Recycling

Key Passport Data

  • Material composition & recycled content
  • Carbon footprint & environmental impact
  • Supply chain due diligence
  • State of health & performance history
  • Repair, reuse & recycling instructions
